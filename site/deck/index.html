I'm a solo builder in Nigeria, building with AI agents. Heads Down gives an idle phone a night job. It digs ORE from your own ORE Automation account while the phone lies face-down. Only a heartbeat signed inside the phone can switch it on. No server can forge one, mine included. 04 / 10

Each rig is an ordinary ORE miner with its own Automation. The user sets the executor to our program's PDA. The program signs ORE's deploy only if, in the same transaction, the precompile verified this rig's key over a message bound to this program, this rig, this shift, this ORE round and a counter that must go up. A random signer can't deploy for your Automation; ORE checks executor equals signer. The crank decides only whether and when; the program computes amount and squares. The program is on mainnet at HDn4…7F9p and its Executor is By3v…Pkge. The three transactions on the slide are from my phone's first shift there: a dig, the BREAK the phone signed when I unlocked it, and the crank's own second attempt with the same heartbeat, which the program refused as stale. That last one was not staged. One key, mine, can still upgrade the program, and nobody outside has audited it.

06 / 10

ORE keeps about 10.5% of the SOL you deploy; that is what mined ORE costs. Fixed per-dig fees make tiny deploys expensive. So the program digs 0.001 SOL at a time on the split squares, only when a Motherlode-aware rule it computes on-chain says mining is the cheaper route. In the backtest the gate stayed shut all night on 30% of nights; those nights just buy. Two things I say out loud. First, the backtest charged 5,000 lamports a dug round and the program on mainnet charges 10,000; the backtest's own sensitivity is about one point per 1,000 lamports at this chunk size, so the small edge has to be measured again at the real fee before anyone quotes it. Second, today the gate is shut on a default build, so for the recorded demo I raised my own ceiling to 1.0 SOL per ORE and cut the budget to 0.005 SOL a shift and 0.035 SOL a week. This is not a money machine; the value is the ritual, the trustless gate and an honest choice of route. There is no revenue today: the planned source is a disclosed Jupiter fee on the buy leg the user signs, and the app does not offer that leg yet.

08 / 10

Only the launcher activity, the tile service and the two widget receivers are exported. The trampoline reads no intent extras. Release builds strip every android.util.Log call, backups are off, RPC is HTTPS-only and the crank uplink WSS-only. The app shows success only after the transaction is confirmed with no error. The phone is a Redmi 14C: accelerometer only, no gyroscope. On mainnet the wallet sends the clock-in itself, and twice the app did not hear that it had landed; it now writes down what it hands the wallet and arms the shift it prepared once the Rig shows it on-chain. That repair has run on the emulator; on the phone the failure has not come back in three clock-ins. The same evening Jupiter refused the sign-in token it had given the app the day before, and the wallet library reported that as a failed connection: the app now reads the wallet's real answer, drops a refused token and asks for one more tap.

09 / 10

No vanity counts, no paid testers, no referral bounties. There are no users yet. Metrics come from on-chain rows only, and anyone can recompute them from the CSV exports. What exists today is checkable: the deploy transaction, the Config, the Executor, four services answering on mainnet, and one phone's shift, transaction by transaction. The shift was six rounds dark. The crank sent the first round's dig too late and it was skipped; the next five landed, 0.001 SOL each on four squares. None of the squares won, so no ORE came out, and each round sent back the 89.1% that ORE gives back for squares that lose. I picked the phone up, it signed a BREAK, I clocked out and took the SOL back, and the wallet received exactly what the screen had said. The digs ran under a ceiling I raised for the demo, with the budget cut to 0.005 SOL a shift. What that evening found, including a dig window that was too short and a clock-in the app did not hear about, is written down in docs/MAINNET.md with what was changed. The next day the phone worked four more short shifts: fifteen digs in fifteen rounds, and the rig's first ORE, 0.0019 of it, sitting unclaimed in its own Miner account. On that small sample it cost about 1.13 SOL per ORE: this is not a money machine, and the page says so.
