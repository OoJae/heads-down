The habit hangs on three things people already do every night: bedtime, the charger and the alarm. If the OS kills the app, the rig goes cold and nothing is spent. On the tenth of October my Redmi did all of this on mainnet except the morning reveal: one approval in Jupiter's wallet, heartbeats signed by a key in the phone's secure hardware, five digs, a BREAK when I picked it up and unlocked it, a clock-out, and the SOL back. Two honest notes. On the phone I tapped Clock in inside the app; the tile's path has run on the emulator. And that evening the app did not hear that its first clock-in had landed, so I approved a second one; the app now finds a landed clock-in on-chain by itself. That repair has run on the emulator; on the phone the failure has not come back in the three clock-ins since. The digs ran under a price ceiling I raised for the demo.

The app

One object on every screen: a dark slab, lit from underneath.

  • The ideaThe app works while you cannot see it. So its mark is the face you never look at: light from under a slab.
  • The objectOne shader draws the slab. It stays level with the real world through the accelerometer: tilt the phone, or drag, to look underneath.
  • The colourOne accent at two temperatures. Ember while the rig is armed or cooling; gold only for a hot rig and for ORE.
  • The rulesOne action, in the thumb zone. The most a shift can place beside it, before the wallet opens. Nothing moves by itself.

The first two: the app on the Redmi 14C, on mainnet, 11 Oct 2026, where this build clocked in three times and dug ten rounds. The last two: the same build on an Android 14 emulator against the local fork of mainnet; the reveal shows the sample night the app offers before a first haul, and says so on screen.

Each rig is an ordinary ORE miner with its own Automation. The user sets the executor to our program's PDA. The program signs ORE's deploy only if, in the same transaction, the precompile verified this rig's key over a message bound to this program, this rig, this shift, this ORE round and a counter that must go up. Honest economics

Spread thin, mining costs more than buying. Gated chunks came out slightly cheaper in a backtest.

08 / 18

ORE keeps about 10.5% of the SOL you deploy; that is what mined ORE costs. Fixed per-dig fees make tiny deploys expensive. So the program digs 0.001 SOL at a time on the split squares, only when a Motherlode-aware rule it computes on-chain says mining is the cheaper route. In the backtest the gate stayed shut all night on 30% of nights; those nights just buy. Two things I say out loud. First, the backtest charged 5,000 lamports a dug round and the program on mainnet charges 10,000; the backtest's own sensitivity is about one point per 1,000 lamports at this chunk size, so the small edge has to be measured again at the real fee before anyone quotes it. Second, today the gate is shut on a default build, so for the recorded demo I raised my own ceiling to 1.0 SOL per ORE and cut the budget to 0.005 SOL a shift and 0.035 SOL a week. This is not a money machine; the value is the ritual, the trustless gate and an honest choice of route. There is no revenue today: the planned source is a disclosed Jupiter fee on the buy leg the user signs, and the app does not offer that leg yet.

09 / 18

We set a promotion rule before training: the forecaster had to beat the simple rule or be demoted. It found real one-hour structure: the cost ratio climbs as the Motherlode pot grows and collapses at each hit. But the program reads the live EMA and pot on every dig for free, and against that the forecast adds nothing measurable: as a gate, +0.03% [−0.13, +0.18] against the live on-chain rule at 0.36 SOL a night. So it explains the night and powers the morning accuracy card; it never decides. The classifier is a 39-feature model over 5-second accelerometer windows. On synthetic tests it keeps at least 99.85% pickup recall with at most 0.2% of bumps breaking a shift; those numbers say nothing about real nights until it is retrained on recordings from this phone. It can only add a break, never remove one. The planner's windows finished 90.6% of simulated shifts, against 57% for a fixed 23:00 to 07:00 window.

Heads Down reuses ORE's custody instead of writing its own vault for mining funds, so we inherit ORE's trust assumption in full, and we say so. The rig key can't require user authentication because it signs while the phone is locked; that is why the wallet-signed caps, not the key, bound the damage. Config changes and governance rotation wait 72 hours in code. Program upgrades do not: the upgrade authority on mainnet is one key, mine, and the threat model says so in plain words. A multisig with a delay comes before the caps go up. Nobody outside the project has audited any of this.
