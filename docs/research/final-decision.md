# FINAL FIVE

## 1. Heads Down (reshaped: the trustless night-shift rig) [heads-down]
PITCH: Put your Seeker face-down on the nightstand or desk and it mines ORE through your own ORE Automation account. Every deploy needs a fresh heartbeat from the phone's hardware Keystore key, verified on-chain by the secp256r1 precompile, so no server, crank or team key can mine your SOL. Pick the phone up and the rig goes cold. Around that core: a morning haul reveal with a mine-or-buy clock-out, rooms with a synchronized Muster, SKR-bonded Stack contests, Gift a Rig, and an on-device AI Foreman that plans each shift.
WHY IT COULD WIN: It has the best public 4x25 score in the field (8.07), the rubric MONOLITH used with four of these same judges. It holds three crowns (Solana Mobile, Ethelsec, community), is top-3 for 6 of 7 judges, has the strongest demo (9.0), and is the only credible path to the ORE match. The red team settled the two decisive objections with evidence. ORE's own deploy.rs and automate.rs show a heartbeat-gated PDA executor is feasible, so no team hot key is needed. Production cost sat below price in 150 of 167 hourly snapshots last week, so 'pay to focus' was overstated. No public repo or store app gates ORE mining on phone state. The habit hangs on sleep and charging, the most reliable daily routines there are, so there is no content pipeline to feed. It also meets Toly's stated conditions for switching his crown and the Align analyst's conditions for switching theirs.
WHY IT MIGHT NOT: On the Align form its weakest columns are AI (4.29) and SKR (5.57), so the Foreman models and the SKR roles have to land for real. Most Seekers are second phones, so 'proof of focus' is not credible; it has to be pitched as a night job for an idle Seeker. RefinORE and Orestack already sell autominers, and focus x ORE appears in the brief, so a rival could ship the same headline. ORE rule churn and Android background limits (Doze, FGS types) threaten the loop. It has the heaviest scope of the finalists: Kotlin system surfaces, a program, a crank and three small models. Its success metric, a Seeker left untouched, also has to be reframed for Solana Mobile's engagement KPIs.
SCORES: Panel: public 8.07 (1st), Align 6.69 (5th), combined 7.38 (2nd). Borda 14, 3 crowns. Dims: demo 9.0, UX 8.0, UI 8.14, innovation 7.71, stickiness 7.57, ecosystem 7.57, SKR 5.57, AI 4.29. ORE-match odds 0.30 (field high), SKR prize 0.07. Red team: wounded, p_first 0.05 and p_top10 0.33, both the field's highest. Projected after the reshape: public ~8.3, Align ~7.5.

## 2. Roll Call (reshaped Pulse: the anonymous verified-Seeker census) [pulse]
PITCH: One question a day, answered from the home-screen widget with a fingerprint: pick an answer, guess the split, then see a synchronized global reveal. Each answer comes from a distinct SGT-verified Seeker but stays anonymous (Semaphore-style ZK nullifiers, with a P-256/secp256r1 fallback), and anyone can recount the tally. Bayesian Truth Serum scoring makes honest answers pay. dApps, DAOs, Solana Mobile and AI agents pay SKR to put questions to the verified panel.
WHY IT COULD WIN: It has the highest combined (7.70) and Align (7.55) scores and is the only candidate in all 7 judges' top-3 (Borda 15). It leads the field on SKR (8.86), UX (8.86) and Ecosystem (8.71), and has the best SKR-prize odds (0.26). Toly, Chase and the Align analyst crowned it. The Wordle-style loop survives with tokens removed, and the custody surface is tiny. It is also the easiest to build without sensors or ORE dependencies.
WHY IT MIGHT NOT: TBD ('predict the crowd', $3M raised, promoted by Solana Mobile to every Seeker with up to $100 each), U$ER (SGT-gated paid polls), HOTSHOT, b4 and Cue already occupy the lane. That hits Beeman, Akshay and Chase directly. The content is the product, and it depends on a solo builder running a daily editorial pipeline. AI stays thin unless the question editor and answer clustering are real. It has no ORE match and a quieter demo. ZK proving on a Dimensity 7300 is risky. The 'first on-chain SGT verifier' claim is false (ORE's claim_seeker came first), and the name PULSE is taken.
SCORES: Panel: public 7.86, Align 7.55, combined 7.70 (1st). Borda 15, 3 crowns (Toly, Chase, Align analyst). Dims: SKR 8.86, UX 8.86, ecosystem 8.71, UI 7.86, stickiness 7.57, innovation 7.57, demo 7.43, AI 4.14. SKR prize 0.26 (field high), ORE match 0. Red team: wounded, p_first 0.03, p_top10 0.22.

## 3. Gateman: clock in with a float (reshaped, no MWA relay) [gateman]
PITCH: Each morning, one Seed Vault double-tap grants a device day key a capped, expiring float through the audited Subscriptions & Allowances program. Untrusted dApps only ever see that float, and every float transaction gets a simulated receipt and a one-tap confirm. Clock-out sweeps it back. An SGT-verified, SKR-bonded Shield registry lets Seekers flag drainers for any wallet to read, and deterministic templates plus on-device AI explain each risk.
WHY IT COULD WIN: It has the best AI (8.29) and ecosystem (8.71) scores, the best Align fit after Pulse (7.56), Mert's crown, and strong Ethelsec respect. Drain fear is the community's loudest pain. The demo lands hard: 'I'm about to get drained, on purpose', then the vault untouched with only $4.80 lost from the float. The Shield registry is a real build of the SKR whitepaper's Ecosystem Curators.
WHY IT MIGHT NOT: Totem Wallet (a Clock In entrant with 291 commits) already ships pre-sign receipts, drainer blocking, an attacker demo dApp and capped session keys. Solflare Guards and Seed Vault's native warnings cover pre-sign screening, and the float's blast radius is roughly a burner wallet's. Activity Tracking scores only the Seed Vault wallet, so farmers have a reason to avoid the float. Use is insurance-shaped (stickiness 5.29), there is no ORE match, and security products won 0 of ~20 prior grand prizes; one audit finding would be fatal.
SCORES: Panel: public 7.07, Align 7.56, combined 7.31 (3rd). Borda 11, 1 crown (Mert). Dims: demo 9.0, ecosystem 8.71, AI 8.29, innovation 7.71, SKR 7.43, UI 6.71, UX 6.29, stickiness 5.29. SKR prize 0.17. Red team: wounded, p_first 0.025, p_top10 0.17.

## 4. Punch (with Airtime folded in as per-second Punch Calls) [punch]
PITCH: The literal clock: punch in with the Quick Settings tile, an NFC tag or a fingerprint, and get paid by the second. Pay arrives as escrowed Solana Payment Channel vouchers that the phone verifies even offline, can be settled at any moment in one transaction, and ticks up on the lock screen. Airtime's voice engine joins as Punch Calls, which bill mentoring, support and QA sessions by the second, so the call becomes the verifiable unit of remote work.
WHY IT COULD WIN: 'They named it Clock In, so we built the clock' is the best theme hook in the field. It would be the first consumer mobile use of Payment Channels (audited, live Sept 3). Every key has a small written worst case, which Ethelsec liked. It scores well on UX (7.86), innovation (7.86) and demo (8.29), fits Toly's payments thesis, and the founder story is real: 30-60 day contractor payment delays in Lagos.
WHY IT MIGHT NOT: Cold start on both sides, because every worker needs a funded payer, and most Seeker owners are not paid hourly in USDC. Judges may read it as B2B payroll rather than a Seeker habit. AI (2.71) and SKR (2.86) sink it on the Align form. Presence is not proof of work, and the voucher signer is a server hot key. There is no ORE match. Airtime's red team found pay-per-second calls a common 2026 hackathon trope (Time.fun and many repos).
SCORES: Panel: public 7.21 (4th), Align 5.61, combined 6.41. No crowns. Dims: demo 8.29, UX 7.86, innovation 7.86, UI 7.29, ecosystem 7.0, stickiness 4.86, SKR 2.86, AI 2.71. Punch itself was not red-teamed. Airtime, the graft source, was: wounded, p_first 0.02, p_top10 0.12, with a recommendation to fold it into Punch.

## 5. STASH x Melt Meter (reshaped STASH with MELT's personal inflation meter) [stash]
PITCH: A daily hard-money ritual. Snap receipts or shelf tags and an on-device vision model builds your private basket and personal inflation rate ('NBS says 15.4%, your basket says 23%'). One Seed Vault double-tap then moves that 'melt' into your own ORE Automation, which mines when the board beats Jupiter's price and buys otherwise. Savings circles, local-currency dig reports and SKR Gift-a-Miner complete it.
WHY IT COULD WIN: It has the safest ORE core: ORE's permissionless executor, no team key. It qualifies for the ORE match, has the third-highest stickiness (6.86) and strong UX (7.71), and matches PiggyVest/Jar habits in the builder's own market. The MELT graft adds real on-device AI and a privacy story Mert would like. It has the lowest execution risk of any ORE option.
WHY IT MIGHT NOT: Innovation is low (4.71): underneath it is an autominer plus DCA, next to refinORE and Orestack. Calling a token about 66% off its high 'savings' in inflation-hit markets is an ethics landmine flagged by Toly, Mert, Chase and Solana Mobile. The autopilot needs a new custody PDA with three CPIs. MELT's thesis numbers are contradicted by current data. Its best mechanic, mine-or-buy, is already grafted into the champion.
SCORES: STASH panel: public 6.46, Align 5.63, combined 6.05. Dims: UX 7.71, stickiness 6.86, innovation 4.71, AI 3.29. ORE match 0.14. MELT panel: public 6.61, Align 7.09 (AI 6.86, innovation 8.43). MELT red team: wounded, p_first 0.015, p_top10 0.10, with the recommendation to fold it into STASH as a personal inflation meter.

# CHAMPION: Heads Down (heads-down)

## DECISION RATIONALE
DECISION. Build Heads Down in the red team's strongest form: a trustless night-shift rig that only the phone can switch on. Graft in STASH's mine-or-buy engine and Gift-a-Miner, and Pulse's synchronized shared reveal. The runner-up is Roll Call, the reshaped Pulse.

1) BOTH RUBRICS.
- Public 4x25 rubric: Heads Down leads the field at 8.07 (Pulse 7.86). MONOLITH used this rubric with four of these judges, and the published site says it governs.
- Align form (AI 20 / SKR 20 / UX 15 / UI 15 / Innovation 15 / Ecosystem 15): Heads Down is only 5th at 6.69. AI (4.29) and SKR (5.57) drag it down, and together they are 40% of that form.
- The spec rebuilds exactly those two columns:
  - AI: three on-device models that do real work. A shift planner. A mine-or-buy forecaster that is scored against reality every morning. A pickup/bump classifier that Stack fairness actually depends on. All of them can only tighten limits the user signed.
  - SKR: four roles, none of them staking. Stack bonds (the Seek pattern that won MONOLITH's SKR prize), solo Focus Bonds, Gift a Rig, and a no-oracle auction that turns forfeited SKR into burned ORE.
- After the reshape I estimate Align at about 7.5 for Heads Down and about 8.0 for a best-case Roll Call. So Pulse keeps a ~0.5 edge on Align, and Heads Down keeps a ~0.3-0.5 edge on the public rubric, which widens after the demo.
- The Align config is probably a stale platform template: identical weights appear on two test records. Confirm this at Radiants office hours.
- Switch rule: if Radiants confirms the Align weights govern AND the day-1 mainnet-fork spike of the PDA executor fails, switch to Roll Call.

2) EACH JUDGE.
- Solana Mobile (Beeman, Akshay), Ethelsec (Voynich, A2nkF) and the community lens already crown Heads Down.
- Toly listed what would move his crown: a heartbeat-gated PDA executor proven on a fork, no rent-seeking fee, honest near-break-even rounds, and a lock-screen hero shot on a real Seeker.
  - The red team read ORE's source and found the executor feasible.
  - The spec replaces the bps fee with a fixed crank-cost fee and moves to an immutable program.
  - A max_production_cost gate plus a morning buy leg replaces the flat-night Motherlode threshold.
- Mert asks who holds the executor key. The answer is 'nobody', with a published worst case: a compromised rig key can only spend your capped budget on ORE at or below the production cost you capped.
- Chase asked for three things, and all three are in the spec:
  - a free no-mining mode (focus-only shifts);
  - retention driven by outcomes (the rhythm report and first-pickup trends);
  - Pulse's shared reveal for rooms (Muster).
- The Align analyst's switch conditions are all met: the classifier as the AI centerpiece, Stack lifting SKR to 7+, and a proven PDA executor.
- Pulse has no equivalent fix for its fatal objection. Solana Mobile already put TBD's 'predict the crowd' in front of every Seeker with up to $100 each, and three of the seven judges sit at Solana Mobile or the Foundation.

3) STICKINESS. Both score 7.57, but the mechanics differ. Heads Down triggers on bedtime, the charger and the alarm, habits that already happen every day, and needs no editorial pipeline. Pulse's retention depends on a solo builder writing a great question every day, the BeReal/HQ failure mode. Heads Down also gives the ~118k dormant Seekers a job instead of fighting over the ~9k active ones. It produces two natural, value-bearing Seed Vault transactions a day, which is what Solana Mobile's Aug 20 re-weighting rewards over memo spam.

4) NOVELTY. The red team found no public repo or store app that gates ORE mining on phone state. The core primitive is new, and it is the demo: a hardware-key heartbeat verified on-chain as the only switch for a third-party ORE executor. RefinORE, Orestack and Oreminer are 24/7 server bots. Pulse's lane is occupied (TBD, U$ER, HOTSHOT, b4, Cue), and its headline technical claim of a 'first on-chain SGT verifier' is false.

5) SECURITY AND CUSTODY.
- Mining funds never leave ORE's own Automation account.
- The only value Heads Down holds is small SKR bonds and gift escrows, both under conservation invariants.
- The crank's worst case is that nothing happens. The registrar only affects capped Stack tables. The upgrade authority is timelocked, then revoked.
- Ethelsec already ranked it first.
- Roll Call's ZK path adds circuit risk. Its non-ZK path leaves the SGT-to-opinion graph that Mert objects to.

6) SOLO + AI FEASIBILITY AT FULL SCOPE. Heads Down is the heavier build: Kotlin system surfaces, a Pinocchio program, a Rust crank and three small models. But every unknown that could kill it can be tested in days 1-3: the fork CPI, secp256r1 with a real Keystore key, and overnight FGS survival on a real Seeker. Each has a fallback with the same worst-case bound. The rest is well-trodden Android/Solana engineering that AI agents handle well; SeekerClaw won MONOLITH as a Kotlin app. A full-scope Roll Call needs Groth16 proving on a Dimensity 7300 plus a daily content engine, and neither its content risk nor its novelty risk can be retired in a week.

7) FOUNDER-MARKET FIT. The Lagos founder can dogfood from night one ('my rig mined while I coded'). The fail-safe design (offline means no mining and no loss) suits power and network cuts. The community line 'my phone dey mine while I sleep' is authentic. There is no personal legal exposure, unlike Alert.

8) PRIZE STACKING.
- Expected value: about $11k for Heads Down, $6.6k for Roll Call, $4.5k for Gateman (see prize_ev).
- Ceiling: $70k for Heads Down (1st + ORE match + SKR) against $40k for the other two.
- Heads Down is the only finalist that can double its placement prize.

9) DEMO POWER. 9.0 against 7.43. The shots are real and honest: the face-down arm, a Solscan 'StaleHeartbeat' rejection when the phone is lifted, the morning haul with mine-or-buy, and a table Stack where a bump does not count and a reach does.

10) DOWNSIDE SCENARIOS.
- ORE rejects the PDA executor: ship a KMS executor with the same bounded worst case and lead with the bound.
- ORE changes its account layouts mid-judging: the circuit breaker stops the program's CPIs, funds stay in users' Automations, and users re-point to a v2 program.
- EV sours: the Foreman keeps rigs cold, the buy leg keeps the ritual alive, and focus-only shifts keep streaks.
- A rival ships 'focus x ORE': the trustless phone gate, Stack and the Foreman differentiate it, and building in public stakes the claim early.
- The Align form governs: Heads Down still lands around 7.5 and stays top-3.
- No Motherlode during the build month: never script one; show near-misses honestly.

WHY IT BEATS THE RUNNER-UP. On the published rubric Heads Down is the stronger 1st-place bet, and the red team gives it the highest p_first and p_top10 in the field. It is the only finalist that can double its prize through the ORE match. Its decisive objections (a team hot key, negative EV) were refuted with source code and data, while Pulse's (the TBD lane and the content burden) are structural. Its demo is a full tier stronger. Roll Call wins only if the Align form truly governs and Heads Down's core CPI fails, and the switch rule covers exactly that case.
