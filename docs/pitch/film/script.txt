Every night your phone lies face-down on its charger, doing nothing. Heads Down gives it a night shift.

Heads Down is your phone's night shift, powered by ORE. Plug in, set your alarm, clock in with one wallet approval, and lay the phone face-down. While it stays down, it digs ORE from your own ORE account, inside limits your wallet signed. Pick it up, and the rig goes cold.

What is new is the referee. Once a minute, the phone signs a heartbeat with a key that cannot leave its secure hardware. Solana checks that signature in the same transaction as the dig, through the secp256r1 precompile. No server can say this phone was face-down. Not even ours. Lift the phone, and it signs a break. The digging stops.

Here is a real shift. Solana mainnet, the twelfth of October 2026, on a Redmi 14C. Forty-one minutes face-down. Thirty-seven rounds dark. Five digs. One approval seals it on-chain as completed. Then the haul: the price paid per ORE, beside the market price.

SKR is the Focus Bond. Ten SKR were locked on that shift. It completed, so the same ten came back. A lost bond is never paid to the team, or to another player. By the program's rule, it is sold for ORE that is burned.

And you come back. Every completed night adds to a streak stored on-chain, with two nights off a month, so one bad night does not end it. The Nights screen keeps every shift, and a share card shows the night with no wallet address.

Heads Down is plain about money. Mining is one route to ORE, not income. The rig digs only while ORE's own cost figure is under a price ceiling, raised for this film so a dig could land. That shift's digs cost 0.894 SOL per ORE, against a market price of 1.057. The rig's first twenty digs cost about 1.13. And the clock-out can buy ORE through Jupiter instead.

All of it has run on Solana mainnet since the tenth of October: ten shifts, thirty-five digs, every transaction public.

Heads Down. Put it down. It digs. Powered by ORE.
